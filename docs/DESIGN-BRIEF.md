# Lumière Dental concept — design brief

## Brand thesis

Lumière makes dental decision-making feel considered: clinical information is examined through warm light, measured framing, and visible context instead of sterile blue-white conventions.

## Audience and conversion goal

Adults evaluating cosmetic or restorative dental care in Beirut. This is a fictional Spline concept demonstration; the conversion is a conversation with Spline about a comparable website, not a clinical booking.

## Emotional target

Precise, warm, calm, and editorial. Visitors should feel guided without being pressured or shown invented outcomes.

## Visual concept

An examination lens: ovals, fine calibration lines, translucent enamel materials, mineral greens, and warm ambient light turn clinical comparison into a careful visual study.

## Typography and palette

Large editorial serif headlines carry confidence; a compact neutral sans handles navigation and explanations. Warm ivory, cream, deep forest, mineral sage, and restrained brass avoid the generic white-and-blue clinic template while retaining strong contrast.

## Grid, shape, and material rules

Use asymmetric editorial grids, one dominant examination stage, long rules, oval lenses, and small calibrated labels. Rounded shapes belong to lenses and compact actions—not generic card containers. Surfaces feel like paper, enamel, and diffused operatory light.

## Media treatment

Use generated dental-study models only: no patient faces, mouths, treatment outcomes, clinicians, rooms, awards, or credentials presented as real. Desktop comparison imagery is wide; mobile uses independently cropped portrait assets. All media is labeled illustrative.

## Motion and interaction

One authored examination moment in the hero and one user-controlled dental-model comparison. Normal scrolling remains intact. Comparison must work by drag, touch, arrow keys, Home/End, and reduced motion.

## Mobile transformation

Mobile shortens the hero, moves the lens below the decision copy, keeps the primary action within the first viewport, uses portrait comparison crops, and replaces hover-only information with always-visible labels.

## Accessibility and content truth

Maintain WCAG AA contrast, visible focus, semantic controls, descriptive labels, reduced motion, and a readable experience without GSAP. Every person, statistic, review, credential, location, and outcome is either verified or removed; this concept uses none as proof.

## Anti-pattern blacklist

- Generic white-and-blue dental cards
- Fabricated patient reviews, ratings, experience, certifications, or results
- Before/after patient photography
- Decorative glass, random gradients, and repeated pills
- Scroll-jacking or comparison controls that only animate on scroll
- Desktop merely stacked on mobile

## Business-exclusive details

1. Enamel translucency is the central material metaphor.
2. The treatment-record comparison uses two consent-safe dental study models.
3. Calibration lines and scan markers connect diagnosis to explanation.
4. Copy centers preservation and informed choice rather than transformation claims.

## Visual acceptance criteria

- The first viewport identifies a fictional Beirut dental concept and a Spline contact path within seconds.
- The hero retains Lumière's warm mineral identity at 1440×900 and 390×844 without large dead zones.
- The comparison is visually legible, user-controlled, keyboard/touch accessible, and labeled as illustrative.
- No unsupported clinic history, result, patient, clinician, certification, review, location, or response-time claim is visible.
- Reduced-motion users receive the final composed hero and a static 50/50 comparison until they operate it.
